Prompt 0 – Chapter Concept Inventory Extractor

Title: Chapter Concept Inventory Generator

Context: You are analyzing a CBSE Class 10 chapter to extract the complete inventory of concepts that must be understood by a student. This concept inventory will serve as the reference list for validating story coverage and learning step coverage in later stages of a storytelling-based teaching system.

Input:
1. Chapter: [Chapter Name]
2. Chapter PDF

Goal: Identify and list all the key concepts that the chapter teaches. The objective is to create a complete concept inventory that represents the knowledge a student must acquire from this chapter. This inventory will later be used to ensure that:
The selected story backbone can explain all concepts
The learning steps cover all concepts
The generated scenes do not miss any concept

Extraction Rules: Extract concepts directly from the chapter content. Include all concepts necessary for a student to fully understand the chapter. Each concept should represent a distinct learning idea. Avoid combining multiple ideas into a single concept. Avoid duplicate or overlapping concepts. If a concept builds on another concept, keep them as separate entries. Keep concept descriptions concise and clear. The goal is to capture the conceptual coverage of the chapter, not to create teaching modules.

Output Format: For each concept provide: Concept Name Short description of the concept (1–2 lines)

Example Output (Illustrative):

1.Sequence - a list of numbers arranged in a specific order based on a pattern.
2.Arithmetic Progression - A sequence in which the difference between consecutive terms remains constant.
3.First Term - The starting value of an arithmetic progression.
4.Common Difference - The constant difference between consecutive terms in an arithmetic progression.
5.nth Term Formula - A formula used to calculate any term in the progression without listing all terms.

Do NOT Create stories Create learning steps Group concepts into modules Solve problems

Focus only on extracting the complete concept inventory of the chapter.

Prompt 1 – Story Backbone Possibilities Creator

Title: Backbone Story Possibilities Creator

Context: I am teaching a CBSE Class 10 student who understands concepts better through real-world stories. The goal is to identify story backbones capable of explaining the entire chapter through one continuous narrative.

Input:

Chapter: [Chapter Name]

Concept Inventory: [Output from Prompt 0 concept inventories]

Goal: Generate around 10 different real-world story backbones that can explain the entire chapter intuitively.

Each story backbone must be evaluated against the Concept Inventory to determine whether the story can naturally explain all required concepts. The objective is to identify full-chapter narrative candidates, not partial metaphors.

Constraints:

Each story must be capable of explaining all concepts listed in the Concept Inventory within one continuous narrative.

If a story cannot naturally explain some concepts:
Clearly indicate which concepts are weakly supported
Indicate whether combining it with another story backbone could solve the gap

Stories must be structurally rich enough to support:
Intuition
Structural understanding
Formula derivation
Application

Stories should:
Map naturally to the chapter concepts
Be suitable for comics, animation, or visual storytelling
Emphasize intuition before formulas

Focus only on conceptual storytelling possibilities.

Terms Definition Coverage Completeness

Allowed values:

Fully Covers

Partially Covers

Requires Combination

Coverage % reflects how naturally the concept can be explained within the story.

Output Format (Per Story Backbone):

1. Story Title
2. Core Narrative Premise - Build a detailed story line with minimum of 10 lines and with proper characters aligning to the story.
3. Concept Coverage Table
For every concept in the Concept Inventory, provide:
Concept → How story explains it → Coverage Completeness → Coverage %
4. Overall Concept Coverage
2–3 lines summarizing how well the story explains the entire chapter. Provide Overall Concept Coverage %.
5. Pedagogical Strength
2–3 lines explaining why this story is effective for teaching. Provide Pedagogical Strength %.
6. Ranking
Sort all identified story backbones:
By the average of both the
Overall Concept Coverage %
Pedagogical Strength %

Final Output Rule:
Provide detailed output only for the top 5-8 stories after ranking.

Do NOT:
Solve textbook problems
Use heavy equations
Create scenes
Produce story scripts

Focus only on identifying strong story backbones capable of explaining the entire chapter.

Prompt 2 – Chapter to Learning Steps Decomposition

Title: Chapter Decomposition Engine for the Selected Story Backbone

Context: I am teaching a CBSE Class 10 student using a selected real-world story as the narrative backbone. The goal is to transform the chapter into a sequence of learning steps that unfold naturally within the chosen story, so that the student experiences the chapter as a smooth and engaging narrative.

Input:

Chapter:
(Insert the chapter name used in previous prompts)

Selected Real-World Story Narrative:
(Insert the selected story backbone output from the previous prompt — specifically the **Core Narrative Premise** of the chosen story)

Concept Inventory:
(Insert the **Concept Inventory output generated from Prompt 0**)

Goal:
Analyze the chapter and decompose it into logically coherent learning steps that together explain the entire chapter in a clear and engaging manner.
The objective is not to restrict the number of steps, but to ensure that:
	Pedagogical clarity is preserved
	The conceptual flow of the chapter is maintained
	No conceptual dependency is broken
	The story backbone can naturally carry the explanation
	The student can follow the story and understand the concepts smoothly
	The number of learning steps should emerge naturally from the structure and the flow of the chapter and the narrative flow of the story.

Decomposition Rules:
Each learning step should represent one meaningful stage in the student's understanding of the chapter.
A concept may require multiple sequential learning steps if that improves clarity and narrative continuity.
Closely related ideas may remain together only if they can be clearly understood within a single learning step.
If a concept requires staged explanation, split it into multiple steps instead of compressing it.
Avoid duplicate learning steps.
If two steps cover overlapping conceptual ground, merge them or clearly differentiate them.
Ensure that all concepts from the chapter are covered at least once.
Do not skip any essential concept.
Avoid unnecessary repetition of the same concept across multiple steps unless the later step extends or deepens the idea.
Maintain conceptual progression where appropriate:
	Intuition
	Structural understanding
	Formula derivation (if applicable)
	Application or reinforcement

Ensure each learning step corresponds to a natural moment in the selected story backbone where the concept can be discovered, observed, or explained.
Ensure that each learning step logically leads to the next, avoiding conceptual jumps that would break narrative continuity.
Each learning step should be expandable into multiple scenes later.

Output Format:
For each learning step provide:
Learning Step ID
Learning Step Title
Concepts Introduced
Bullet points listing the concepts explained in this step

Narrative Moment:
A detailed description of minimum 5+ lines covering:
Story Context
Trigger Event
Character Interaction
Concept Discovery Setup
Transition to Next Learning Step

Concept Coverage Validation:
After generating all learning steps, provide a Concept Coverage Table.
For each concept from the Concept Inventory provide:
Concept → Learning Step(s) where it appears → Coverage Completeness → Coverage %
Coverage Completeness Allowed Values:
	Fully Covered
	Partially Covered
	Requires Additional Step

Coverage % indicates how clearly the concept is represented across the learning steps.

Do NOT:
Create scenes
Write story dialogue
Provide detailed teaching explanations
Solve textbook problems

Focus only on building a clear sequence of learning steps that combines the chapter structure with the story backbone in a natural and understandable flow.

Goal:
Generate a complete set of scenes that explain the current learning step concepts through storytelling.

Scene Count Rule:
Do NOT restrict the number of scenes.

Generate as many scenes as required for the learning step to naturally explain the concepts and complete the story progression.

A learning step may contain:
- a small number of scenes if the concept is simple
- many scenes if the concept requires gradual discovery

Scene count should emerge naturally from the teaching and storytelling requirements rather than being fixed.

The scenes must:
start naturally from the previous learning step narrative
follow a clear story arc
explain the concepts introduced in the current learning step
end with a smooth transition toward the next learning step

Scene Structure
Scenes must follow a logical storytelling progression using these phases:
hook
observation
investigation
discovery
explanation
application
reinforcement
transition and etc..

Not all phases are mandatory, but the story should flow naturally across them.

Scene Design Rules:
Concepts introduced in the learning step must appear in at least one scene.
Dialogue must remain natural conversation and descriptive.
Scenes must remain visually descriptive to support animation or comics or the dialogue.
Story characters must remain consistent with the story backbone.
The final scene must naturally transition toward the next learning step.
Maintain continuity with the previous learning step narrative.
Introduction Rule:
If the Previous Learning Step is empty or not provided, the first scene of the current learning step must begin with an environmental introduction before characters begin interacting.

The scene should first establish:
- the environment
- the atmosphere
- the situation the student is entering

Characters should appear naturally after this environment introduction, similar to how films first show the setting before dialogue begins.
Dialogue should not start immediately at the beginning of the scene.

Pedagogical Rules:
Build intuition before formulas.
Avoid introducing formulas before conceptual understanding.
Use discovery-based explanation where possible.
Keep explanations beginner-friendly.
Avoid mixing multiple unrelated concepts in one scene.

Output Rules:
Output ONLY valid JSON.
Follow the JSON schema exactly.
Do not include explanations outside JSON.
